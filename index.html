        Tips for Grown-ups

        • Pick a calm moment. Practise when nobody is rushing out of the door.
        • Start on the table. A shoe on a table (toe pointing away) is easier than one on a foot. Move to their own foot once it clicks.
        • Sit beside, not opposite. Seen from the front, everything is mirrored. Sit next to or behind your child so you share the same view.
        • Two colours help. Colour half of one lace with a permanent marker, or tie two different laces together, so "left" and "right" are easy to name.
        • Choose easy laces. Longer, flat, cotton laces grip better than short, round, slippery ones.
        • Do the last step first. Tie everything yourself and let them do the final pull. Then hand over one more step each time (backward chaining).
        • Use the rhyme. Saying the words out loud helps children remember the order.
        • Praise trying. Most children master this between ages 5 and 7, and it can take weeks. Wobbly bows still count!
